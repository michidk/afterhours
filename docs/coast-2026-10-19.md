# Coast shaping, 19 October 2026

The sea coast looked jagged: the land edge ran in cell-sized steps along the terrain grid, and the sand and grass met in a sawtooth. Seed 713 was photographed before and after the changes below (`scripts/street-photos.ts`, sites in [sites.json](screenshots/coast-2026-10-19/sites.json)). The photos tint everything below the waterline as sea, but show no waves, foam or shadows.

## Causes

- **A plateau ending in a drop.** `shapeCoast` rewrote each coastal cell on its own thresholds. Cells on a gentle shore became a near-level shelf 0.35 m above sea level, and the next cell fell below the sea. The waterline sat where that shelf ended, so it traced the shelf's cell-aligned edge. The terrain also crossed the waterline at a median of 1.5 m per 8 m cell, and at over 5 m for one in ten.
- **A colour switch at one height.** Vertex colours were sand below one height and green above it. Blended across 8 m triangles, that draws a sawtooth along the cell edges wherever the two meet.
- **Harbours.** Quays followed the seaward direction at any angle, and the basin dredge dug straight through the land beside the quay. Both left vertical walls that stepped with every cell.

## Changes

- **`shapeCoast` (`mapgen/terrain.ts`).** The signed distance to the coast, positive on land, is blurred, which turns the coastline into a clean curve without any cell structure. The distance then sets one beach profile along all of it: a 6% ramp above the waterline and a 10% shelf below it. The profile holds out to 20 m from the coast and eases back into the original terrain by 60 m. The coast sits exactly at sea level. A unit test builds a zigzagging plateau coast, checks that each row crosses sea level once, that the crossings wobble by under 0.35 m, and that land far from the coast is untouched. It fails on the old code.
- **Terrain colour (`client/world/terrain.ts`).** Colour now blends smoothly with height above the waterline: wet sand, sand, grass, then rock above 90 m.
- **Harbours (`scripts/mapgen-features.ts`).** Quays are snapped to the terrain axes, so each face is one straight line. The basin dredge only cuts land in the corridor in front of the quay, and deepens water elsewhere. The apron eases out over 24 m. Sea depth near a site only has to reach 0.5 m, since the basin is dredged to 4 m anyway.
- **Racetrack.** The track avoided ground under 1.5 m above the sea, which was a thin strip on the old steep coast but a band tens of metres wide on the new beaches. It now needs 0.8 m. The lap is 584 m (608 m before).

Three tests needed attention because the new coast changes the island:

- **Road grades.** A short street between two junction plateaus reached a grade of 0.168 against its 0.15 limit. The profile solver assumed at least 1 m between the plateaus of an edge; it now allows 0.5 m, and its grade sweeps run until nothing is clamped instead of six times.
- **Building pads.** The test sampled the cell centre, but a road corridor in the same cell keeps its own profile, so the centre can sit lower than the pad. The test now requires at least one corner of the cell to carry the pad.
- **Racetrack lap.** Fixed by the racetrack change above.

## Remaining

- Rivers and lakes still have blocky, dark bank walls where a channel is cut into the terrain, and a few river ends stop in a square block. They are a separate feature and untouched here.
- Quay faces are vertical walls by design; at 8 m grid resolution their ends still show a one-cell step.
- The beach is a uniform 6% slope. A real coast varies, and cliffs reach the sea only where the original terrain was already steeper than the profile can hide (the steepest coast step is still 26 m per cell).
